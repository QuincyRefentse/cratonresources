Strategy Two · Trading
Market fragmentation as structural opportunity.

Across SADC agricultural and commodity markets, supply is available but dispersed. Producers are fragmented, small-scale farmers, artisanal miners, dispersed co-operatives, and buyers are distant, institutional. The gap between them is a structural arbitrage that rewards whoever builds the aggregation layer.

Supply SideProducer aggregation — small-scale farmers, artisanal miners, co-operatives consolidated into bankable volume.
Demand SideBuyer access — institutional off-takers, processors, export buyers connected to verified SADC supply.
The EdgeTechnical pricing — polymetallic concentrates, off-spec materials, complex agri commodities.
The Corridor Model: fragmented supply + aggregation + verified demand = a corridor. We build the structure that neither side could create alone.

The Integrated Thesis · why three divisions become one edge

Advisory feeds Trading — Borrowers who need working capital against commodity inventory become trading counterparties. The credit relationship and the trading relationship reinforce each other.

Trading feeds Advisory — Two decades of commodity trading surfaces credit opportunities before they become visible to financial advisors. The origination network is built on trading relationships, not database searches.

Both feed Capital — Deal flow from Advisory and trading positions from the Trading division create the investment pipeline for Capital. Third-party investors access a SADC origination capability they cannot build themselves.
